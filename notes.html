What changed

  • Your photographs are in. The three lycra beanbags in the sensory room now sit on the lycra section, the school hall is on the home page, and your lap pad and snake photos are on the weighted products page.
  • No photo of you. The empty box is gone. If you ever change your mind it is a two-minute job to add one.
  • Snake and lap pad now carry the descriptions from your shop — sizes, weights and colours.
  • XXX-Large is €425, the colour range stays as it was, and the mini and small beanbags are left off.
  • Your home page words are in: “not a pillow”, rough and tumble, running into it from a distance, jumping from a trampoline or swing, the fleece and lining that survive real life, hand-made in Cork and matched to each child.
  • Your fleece-versus-lycra comparison is on the beanbags page as a small table under the two fabrics. Both descriptions are there, as you wondered — the cards say what each one is, the table says which to pick. Easy to drop if it feels like too much.
  • Care and washing now follows your two documents: the lycra cover inside a fleece cover, the zip at the bottom of the beanbag, the duvet cover for weighted blankets, the do's and don'ts.
  • Returns and cancellations are written down on the ordering page: made to order, so not returnable for a change of mind, but anything wrong will be put right — and no return in over twenty years.
  • Safety now explains the 2017 testing in plain words and links to your signed certificate and the three test reports from your current site.
  • Delivery is Ireland only, free in Cork city, €10–15 by courier elsewhere. Payment is Revolut, bank transfer, or cash on delivery in Cork.
  • Testimonials. Twelve from your Testimonials page are now on the About page — therapists, parents and schools. The full list is long, so this is a selection; say if there is one you would swap in.

Still open

Short ones. A phone call would cover the lot.

  1. The lycra price

    You wrote that lycra beanbags are “20 euro more”. More than the fleece price, or more than the €250 and €285 your website shows for Large and X-Large lycra? The draft still shows those two figures until you say.

  2. The “SB+” logo

    The picture you sent is a photo of a screen, which is too blurry to use on a website. If you have the original file — the designer would have sent it, probably ending in .png, .svg, .ai or .pdf — please forward it. Also: is the business now called “Sensory Beanbags + More”? That would change the name at the top of every page.

  3. The 15-year guarantee

    You mentioned a 15-year guarantee on the fleece and lining. Before putting that on the site it is worth being clear what it covers — the fabric and stitching, presumably, but not the beads settling or normal wear. A line or two from you and it goes on.

  4. Courier cost

    You said €10 or €15. Does that depend on the size of the beanbag, or on where it is going? The site currently says “€10–15” without explaining.

  5. The photo under your title

    In your document you put the purple beanbag with the snake on it directly under “Sensory beanbags that help children settle”. Did you mean it as the main photo on the home page? It is on the weighted products page at the moment, with the snake. It can be the home page photo instead, or as well.

  6. Naming the school

    You labelled the hall photo “Douglas Boys school”. It is on the home page, but the school is not named. Would you like it named? If so, it is worth checking the school is happy to be.

  7. The weighted blanket certificate

    Your certificate page says the weighted blanket's paperwork was “being assembled” in 2017. Was it ever finished? The site says “CE approved” at the bottom of every page, and the certificate we have covers the beanbags.

  8. The online shop

    You asked about Stripe. Nathan will come back to you on this separately — it is a bigger conversation than a web page.

  9. Google reviews

    A Google Business listing can show reviews without showing your address — you can mark it as a business that serves customers at their location. When it exists, a link to it can go on the About page next to the testimonials. The schools you mentioned would be worth asking; a line from a school is worth a lot to another school.
