Common questions

Which size beanbag should I get?

Large and X-Large suit most homes — Large if space is tight, X-Large if you have the room. The XX-Large and XXX-Large are for when space is not an issue: they have more give, which makes them better for sprawling, jumping into and rolling about in, and big enough for a few children at once. Full dimensions are on the sensory beanbags page.

What is the difference between the fleece and the lycra?

Fleece is the original: warm, tactile, hard-wearing and machine washable, with a four-way stretch inner lining. The lycra version has stretch lycra on both the lining and the outer cover, which feels cool against the skin and stretches almost without limit — it suits children who actively seek sensory input. The lycra beanbags are made on request, so email or call to order one.

How do I wash a beanbag?

The whole beanbag washes as one unit, beads and lining included. The cover also comes off easily on its own. In an industrial machine you can put the whole thing in as one piece; in a domestic machine, take some of the beads out first so it fits.

Wash on a gentle cycle with a small amount of mild detergent. Do not use fabric softener. Line dry, or machine dry on a short cycle — check it every 10 minutes, as it dries very quickly.

Will I need to top up the beads?

Top up as required — roughly once every two or three years. In practice most people go a good deal longer than that before they bother.

How much weight should a weighted blanket or lap pad be?

For lap pads, around 5% of the child's body weight is the usual starting point — a three-year-old is typically well suited to 1.5 lb or 2 lb. For blankets for young children, 2 lb or 2.5 lb at roughly 97 × 92 cm is often plenty. Please check with your occupational therapist before ordering. There is more detail on the weighted products page.
