Common questions

Which size beanbag should I get?

Large and X-Large suit most homes — Large if space is tight, X-Large if you have the room. The XX-Large and XXX-Large are for when space is not an issue: they have more give, which makes them better for sprawling, jumping into and rolling about in, and big enough for a few children at once. Full dimensions are on the sensory beanbags page.

What is the difference between the fleece and the lycra?

Fleece is the original and by far the most popular: warm, cosy, hard-wearing and machine washable, with a four-way stretch inner lining. It is the one for standing jumps, diving and firm resistance. The lycra version has stretch lycra on both the lining and the outer cover, which feels cool and slippery and yields to every movement — better for sinking in and lying down than for crashing. There is a side-by-side comparison on the sensory beanbags page. The lycra beanbags are made on request, so email or call to order one.

How do I wash a beanbag?

A fleece beanbag washes as one unit, beads and lining included, and the cover is easily removed for washing. A Medium beanbag can be washed in your washing machine and tumble dried as one unit — the beads stay in the bag during the washing and drying process. For larger sizes in a domestic machine, take some of the beads out first so it fits. Gentle cycle, a small amount of mild detergent, no fabric softener. Line dry, or machine dry on a short cycle, checking every 10 minutes.

A lycra cover should be washed inside a fleece cover, pillowcase or duvet cover, because the elastane can tangle and tear in the machine. Full instructions, including how to put the beanbag back together safely, are on the sensory beanbags page.

Will I need to top up the beads?

Top up as required — roughly once every two or three years. In practice most people go a good deal longer than that before they bother. A less tightly packed beanbag is actually better for sensory use, so there is no hurry.

How much weight should a weighted blanket or lap pad be?

For lap pads, around 5% of the child's body weight is the usual starting point — a three-year-old is typically well suited to 1.5 lb or 2 lb. For blankets for young children, 2 lb or 2.5 lb at roughly 97 × 92 cm is often plenty. Please check with your occupational therapist before ordering. There is more detail on the weighted products page.

Are the beanbags safe?

Yes. In 2017 Julie sent her products and fabrics to an independent testing laboratory in the UK. The beanbags and weighted blankets were tested to the European toy safety standard for fire (EN 71-2), and the fleece and lycra fabrics for chemical safety (EN 71-3). Everything passed, and the beanbags carry a signed CE Declaration of Conformity to the Toy Safety Directive.

The beads are harmful if swallowed, which is why the inner lining's zip is designed to sit at the bottom of the beanbag, out of reach, once it is assembled. There is a note on the sensory beanbags page on putting it back together the same way after washing.
